Part 2: Method & Architecture (Speaker 2)
Slide 6: The Pipeline (Method Guideline 1)
(Speaker 2): "This is the architecture from Figure 2. The key idea is propagation. The output from one coarse stage, Y^(s-1), becomes the input for the next, finer stage. A crucial detail is that the same Segmentation and Refinement network weights are shared at every step, making it efficient."

Slide 7: A Single Step: The Two Inputs
(Speaker 2): "Let's look at a single step, Level s. The network receives two simultaneous inputs:

Y_bar_p^(s-1): The Global Context. This is the cumulative, coarse map from the previous stage.
O_bar_p^s: The Local Detail. This is the brand new prediction, made only at this current, higher-resolution scale."
Slide 8: The Refinement Module (Concept)
(Speaker 2): "How do we merge these? We don't just average them. We use a Refinement Module that intelligently decides which pixels to update, based on uncertainty. It's a selective update, not a blind merge."

Slide 9: The Selection Logic (Method Guideline 1)
(Speaker 2): "This is the 'magic' from Figure 3. The formula is Q = Y^u * (1 - R^u). This score Q is high only if the old map (Y) is uncertain AND the new map (R) is certain. The system then selects the top-k pixels with the highest Q score (e.g., 65,536 pixels) and replaces them. This guarantees stability."

Slide 10: MagNet-Fast
(Speaker 2): "Because the full process is slow—processing 85 patches on Cityscapes (1+4+16+64)—the authors propose MagNet-Fast. It only refines the top-k most uncertain patches (e.g., top 3) at each new scale. This reduces the workload from 85 patches to just 7 (1+3+3), making it practical."
